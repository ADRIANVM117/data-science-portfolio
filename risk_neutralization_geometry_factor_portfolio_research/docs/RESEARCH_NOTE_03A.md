# Portfolio Geometry #03A

## From Economic Ideas to an Auditable Quantamental Representation

**Status:** **RESEARCH NOTE #03A — CLOSED.** **STAGE 1C — CLOSED.**
Stage 2 has not started.

**Relationship to the project:** Portfolio Geometry #01 established the
geometric interpretation of factor neutralization; #02 established its
numerical implementation; #03 moves the experiment to real equity data. This
note narrates how the frozen raw economic representation was obtained. It does
not replace [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md), the canonical experiment design,
or [CHARACTERISTICS.md](CHARACTERISTICS.md), the detailed audit record.

## Abstract

Geometric portfolio decomposition is only interpretable if the portfolio being
decomposed has a defensible economic representation.

This research note develops the representation layer required for a later
experiment that will decompose a quantamental equity portfolio into components
aligned with, and orthogonal to, selected systematic risk exposures.

Rather than beginning with portfolio weights, the analysis works backward from
economic ideas to auditable raw characteristics.

Value, Quality, Growth, and Momentum are treated as research objects whose
measurement requires an explicit economic interpretation, point-in-time
information set, accounting or market-data construction, provenance, and
failure conditions.

Candidate representations are audited before any downstream portfolio
performance or geometric outcome is observed.

The resulting raw representation contains seven components:

\[
X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}}
=
\left[
\mathrm{EarningsYield},
\mathrm{Profitability},
\mathrm{CashRealization},
\mathrm{RevenueCAGR}_{3Y},
\mathrm{PositiveAnnualGrowthFraction}_{3Y},
R_{12M},
R_{1M}
\right].
\]

The audit also produces negative results.

Latest-restated fundamental history is rejected as a historical information
model; adjusted prices are unsuitable for point-in-time Market Cap when their
share basis is incompatible with reported shares; raw operating-cash-flow-to-
earnings requires an economically motivated transformation; and no
sufficiently coherent cross-sector representation of Balance-Sheet Resilience
is retained.

Other constructions, including 12-1 Momentum, are not rejected but are not
selected because they answer a different economic question.

The resulting \(X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}}\) is intentionally not yet
\(X_{\mathrm{char}}\):

\[
X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}} \ne X_{\mathrm{char}}.
\]

No normalization, cross-sectional transformation, aggregation, portfolio
mapping, risk-exposure matrix, geometric projection, or alpha claim is
introduced.

The note therefore establishes the economic and point-in-time representation
required before returning to the original geometric question:

> How much of a quantamental portfolio's structure remains outside the span of
> selected known risk exposures?

## 1. From Geometry Back to Representation

Experiments #01–02 establish the eventual decomposition:

\[
w_{\mathrm{raw}} = w_{\parallel} + w_{\perp}.
\]

Before asking what part of a portfolio survives projection, Experiment #03
must establish which economic information will eventually generate
\(w_{\mathrm{raw}}\). Its central question remains:

> How much of the structure of a quantamental portfolio is independent of
> selected known risk exposures?

Portfolio Geometry #01 and #02 established the geometric interpretation and
numerical implementation of factor neutralization. Given a portfolio
\(w_{\mathrm{raw}}\) and a subspace \(S_{\mathrm{risk}}\) generated by selected
exposure directions, the portfolio can be decomposed as

\[
w_{\mathrm{raw}} = w_{\parallel} + w_{\perp},
\]

where

\[
w_{\parallel} = P_S w_{\mathrm{raw}},
\]

and

\[
w_{\perp} = (I - P_S) w_{\mathrm{raw}}.
\]

The first component represents the part of the portfolio aligned with the
selected exposure space, while the second represents the component orthogonal
to those directions. The purpose of Experiment #03 is therefore not simply to
produce a neutral portfolio, but to study how the structure of a quantamental
portfolio decomposes relative to known risk exposures.

This immediately creates a prior research problem. Before asking what portion
of a portfolio survives the projection, we must first establish what economic
information generated that portfolio.

In Experiment #03, companies are intended to be represented through
economically interpretable characteristics such as Value, Quality, Growth and
Momentum. These characteristics will eventually form a matrix
\(X_{\mathrm{char}}\), from which a deterministic portfolio rule \(g(\cdot)\)
will produce

\[
w_{\mathrm{raw}} = g(X_{\mathrm{char}}).
\]

However, neither the names of the characteristics nor the availability of
vendor fields is sufficient to define \(X_{\mathrm{char}}\). A P/E ratio
supplied by a data provider, for example, does not by itself establish what
this experiment means by Value. The economic interpretation, mathematical
construction, underlying information set and treatment of unavailable or
ambiguous observations must first be specified.

Consequently, the first empirical stage of Experiment #03 moved temporarily
backward from portfolio geometry to economic representation. Rather than
immediately constructing \(w_{\mathrm{raw}}\) and projecting it, the research
first asked how Value, Quality, Growth and Momentum could be represented using
auditable point-in-time information.

This distinction matters because the eventual geometric question is:

> How much of the structure of a quantamental portfolio is independent of
> selected known risk exposures?

and answering it requires keeping two objects conceptually separate:

\[
X_{\mathrm{char},t}
\]

and

\[
F_{\mathrm{risk},t}.
\]

The first represents the economic characteristics from which the portfolio is
constructed. The second represents the exposure directions relative to which
that portfolio will eventually be decomposed. They are deliberately different
research objects.

Research Note #03A documents the construction of the first of these objects.
It does not yet construct \(X_{\mathrm{char}}\) itself. Instead, it records
the methodological path from economic concepts to the frozen raw components
that will later enter that matrix:

```text
economic idea
-> candidate measure
-> point-in-time audit
-> stress test
-> accept / modify / reject
-> X_char_raw
```

Only after that representation is established can the experiment proceed
toward \(X_{\mathrm{char}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{raw}}\), the risk-exposure
subspace, and ultimately the geometric decomposition.

## 2. Research Governance: A Characteristic Is a Research Object

The governing path was:

```text
economic idea
  -> candidate measure
  -> PIT / data audit
  -> accounting or market-data stress test
  -> accept / modify / reject
  -> frozen raw representation
```

A vendor field is evidence, not an automatic economic characteristic. The
seven components below are research outcomes of this process, not arbitrary
factor selections.

A characteristic in this experiment is not defined by the existence of a
convenient field, ratio, or factor label. Terms such as Value, Quality, Growth,
and Momentum describe economic ideas, but they do not uniquely determine how
those ideas should be measured.

Consider Value. Calling a company “cheap” may refer to its book value relative
to price, earnings relative to market capitalization, cash generation relative
to price, or enterprise value relative to an operating quantity. Each
candidate embeds a different economic interpretation. Selecting one therefore
constitutes a research decision rather than a data-retrieval decision.

Experiment #03 consequently treats each characteristic as a research object
whose definition must be established before it can enter the portfolio
construction process. For every characteristic, the methodology requires an
economic interpretation, mathematical definition, underlying raw fields,
transformations, treatment of missing or exceptional observations, and
explicit observation and availability dates.

This leads to the research sequence used throughout Stage 1C:

\[
\text{Economic idea}
\rightarrow \text{Candidate representation}
\rightarrow \text{PIT feasibility}
\rightarrow \text{Stress test}
\rightarrow \text{Accept / Modify / Reject}.
\]

The order matters. The economic question comes before the available field. A
candidate is then tested against the information that would actually have been
available at the historical decision time. Only after surviving that audit can
it become part of the frozen raw representation.

This governance changes the meaning of failure. A missing value is not
automatically a defect to be repaired. If the information required to construct
a characteristic was not demonstrably available at the decision time, or if
competing observations cannot be resolved without introducing information from
the future, then `NA` can be the correct research result. The methodology
therefore prohibits silent imputation, silent dropping of observations, and
transformations introduced merely to make the dataset complete.

The same principle applies to economically plausible measures that fail
empirical scrutiny. A candidate may have a reasonable interpretation and still
be rejected if its accounting construction is not sufficiently comparable
across firms, if its point-in-time provenance cannot be established, or if its
numerical behavior contradicts the economic meaning assigned to it. Rejection
does not imply that the underlying economic concept is irrelevant; it means
that the specific representation did not satisfy the requirements of this
experiment.

Importantly, these decisions are made before observing downstream portfolio
performance. Characteristics are not selected because they later produce a
higher Sharpe ratio, more attractive backtest, stronger alpha, or more
convenient geometric decomposition. Stage 1C asks whether the representation
is economically coherent, point-in-time feasible, reproducible, auditable, and
sufficiently distinct for the experiment — not whether it predicts returns.

The resulting frozen characteristic is therefore more than a numerical column.
It is a tuple of economic meaning, mathematical construction, information set,
provenance, and explicit failure conditions:

\[
\mathrm{Characteristic} =
\left(
\begin{array}{l}
\text{economic meaning},\\
\text{measurement rule},\\
\text{information set},\\
\text{provenance},\\
\text{failure conditions}
\end{array}
\right).
\]

This is a conceptual summary of the research governance, not a new
mathematical object or additional methodological requirement.

This perspective becomes essential in the following sections. Several
candidates that initially appeared reasonable were subsequently modified or
rejected — not because another specification performed better, but because the
audit exposed incompatibilities between the intended economic meaning and the
information that could actually be reconstructed.

## 3. The Information Set Comes Before the Characteristic

The frozen decision timing is conceptually:

\[
\mathcal{I}_t = \{\text{eligible fundamentals before open }t,
\; \text{market information through close }t-1\}.
\]

Fundamentals use the conservative SEC acceptance-based XNYS eligibility
convention. Amendments enter prospectively; unresolved ambiguity is `NA`; and
later restated history never rewrites an earlier information set. Reported
shares are discrete reported states, not continuously observed daily truth.
Fundamental and market clocks remain distinct.

A historical characteristic is meaningful only relative to the information
that was available when the portfolio decision could actually have been made.
Knowing that a company eventually reported a value is not sufficient. The
research must establish when that information became eligible to enter the
decision process.

Experiment #03 therefore defines every portfolio decision at the regular-
session open of an XNYS trading day \(t\). At that instant, the admissible
information set is

\[
\mathcal{I}_t = \{
\text{fundamental information eligible before open }t,
\; \text{market information through close }t-1
\}.
\]

This convention separates two clocks that are easy to conflate. Market
information evolves through trading sessions, while fundamental information
becomes available through discrete corporate filings. A characteristic that
combines both sources — such as Earnings Yield — must respect both clocks
simultaneously.

### Fundamental information

For SEC fundamentals, the accounting period to which an observation refers is
not its availability date. A fiscal quarter may end months before the
corresponding filing becomes observable to the investor. Using the fiscal-
period end as the historical availability date would therefore insert
information into the past.

The experiment instead uses the filing's SEC `acceptanceDateTime` as the
observable filing event and applies a deliberately conservative eligibility
convention:

\[
\text{SEC fact becomes portfolio-eligible at the open of the first eligible
XNYS regular session strictly after acceptance.}
\]

This convention does not claim that `acceptanceDateTime` is the exact instant
at which every market participant could access the filing. It is an operational
point-in-time rule chosen to avoid treating the filing as usable before the
next complete eligible decision opportunity. The project documentation
explicitly distinguishes this convention from a claim about exact SEC public
dissemination timing.

This creates a distinction between at least three economically different
dates:

\[
\text{observation period}
\ne \text{filing acceptance}
\ne \text{portfolio eligibility}.
\]

Collapsing them into a single date would create look-ahead bias before any
model or backtest had even been constructed.

### Market information

Because the portfolio decision occurs at the open of session \(t\), market
information from that same session is not yet known. Prices and other market
quantities used by the characteristic layer are therefore restricted to
information available through the close of session \(t-1\).

Consequently, a variable such as Earnings Yield at decision time \(t\) is not
simply earnings divided by market capitalization. It is constructed from
earnings that have passed the fundamental eligibility rule and market
information that has passed the separate market cutoff. The resulting number
is therefore indexed not only by economic content, but by an explicit
historical information set.

### Amendments and historical vintages

The same logic determines how later amendments are treated. An amended filing
may change what is known after its acceptance, but it cannot rewrite what the
historical researcher knew before that event. Amendments are therefore applied
prospectively rather than retrospectively. The reconstruction explicitly
rejects a latest-restated-history approach.

This is particularly important when reconstructing quarterly flows from
cumulative filings. For example,

\[
Q_2 = H1 - Q_1, \qquad
Q_3 = 9M - H1, \qquad
Q_4 = FY - 9M
\]

is permitted only when the underlying observations are compatible in concept,
fiscal structure, unit, provenance, and information vintage. An unresolved
material disagreement does not authorize the researcher to select whichever
observation produces the cleaner series; the quarter remains unavailable.

### Missingness as an information result

This leads to a central consequence of the PIT framework:

\[
\text{Unavailable or unresolved information at }t \rightarrow \mathrm{NA},
\]

not retrospective repair.

An `NA` can therefore contain information about the limits of the historical
information set. It may indicate that four valid quarters were not yet
available, that duplicate observations could not be resolved without an
arbitrary choice, that an accounting concept was incompatible across vintages,
or — as later observed in Market Cap — that the price and reported-share bases
became incompatible after a stock split.

Filling such observations using information discovered later would make the
dataset numerically more complete while making the historical experiment less
truthful.

The point-in-time layer is therefore not merely a preprocessing step. It
defines the boundary of the experiment:

\[
\mathrm{Characteristic}_{i,t} = f(\mathcal{I}_t).
\]

Every characteristic developed in the following sections must be
reconstructible from \(\mathcal{I}_t\), or explicitly return `NA`. Only after
establishing this boundary does it make sense to ask whether the resulting
representation is economically useful.

## 4. Value — Economic Generation Relative to Market Valuation

The Value investigation moved from candidate measures through PIT Net Income
and PIT Market Cap to the frozen raw component:

\[
\mathrm{EarningsYield} = NI\_TTM\_PIT / \mathrm{MarketCap\_PIT}.
\]

Historical PIT Market Cap uses raw close with compatible reported-share basis.
Adjusted close was rejected for this object because historical reported shares
and adjusted prices have incompatible split bases.

The economic objective of Value in Experiment #03 is to represent current
economic generation relative to market valuation. This definition deliberately
precedes the choice of a ratio. Book-to-Price, Earnings-to-Price,
Cash-Flow-to-Price, and Enterprise-Value-based measures may all be described
as Value measures, but they encode different economic questions.

The initial candidate set therefore included Book-to-Price, Earnings Yield,
Cash-Flow-to-Price, and Enterprise-Value-based representations. The research
prioritized measures connecting current economic generation to the valuation
assigned by the market. Cash generation was initially attractive, but using it
as the Value representation would create substantial conceptual overlap with
the Cash Realization dimension subsequently investigated under Quality.
Earnings Yield was therefore selected for this experiment as the candidate
representation:

\[
\mathrm{EarningsYield}_{i,t} =
\frac{NetIncome^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t}}{MarketCap^{PIT}_{i,t}}.
\]

This selection was not based on subsequent return performance and does not
imply that Earnings Yield is a universally superior Value measure. It defines
what Value means within this experiment.

The apparent simplicity of the ratio hides two separate point-in-time
reconstruction problems.

### 4.1 Reconstructing the numerator

SEC `NetIncomeLoss` observations do not arrive as a ready-made historical TTM
series. The audited filings contain direct quarters, cumulative half-year and
nine-month observations, annual values, comparative observations, duplicates,
and later amendments.

Quarterly earnings can therefore be reconstructed only when compatible
observations permit identities such as:

\[
Q_2 = H1 - Q_1, \qquad
Q_3 = 9M - H1, \qquad
Q_4 = FY - 9M.
\]

Compatibility requires more than arithmetic. The underlying observations must
correspond in issuer, concept, unit, fiscal structure, period nesting, vintage,
and provenance. Direct and reconstructed quarters can be compared as a
consistency test, but an unresolved material disagreement produces `NA` rather
than an arbitrary choice. A valid TTM value requires four valid and distinct
quarterly observations. Amendments affect the series prospectively only.

The resulting numerator is therefore not simply “latest annual earnings.” It
is

\[
NI^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t},
\]

a trailing earnings quantity that can be reconstructed from the information
set available at decision time \(t\).

### 4.2 The denominator is not simply price times shares

The denominator introduced a different problem. A point-in-time market
capitalization appears to require only:

\[
MarketCap_{i,t} = Price_{i,t-1} \times Shares_{i,t}.
\]

But historical price and historical reported shares must also be expressed on
a compatible share basis.

The split audit exposed this issue directly. Around Apple's 4-for-1 split in
August 2020, the historical adjusted-close series had already been
retrospectively expressed on a split-adjusted basis, while the latest
point-in-time SEC shares observation available before the post-split filing
still represented the pre-split share basis. Multiplying those two quantities
generated a mechanically incorrect historical market capitalization.

Using raw close solves the retrospective adjustment problem before the split,
but it creates another transition problem: on the split date the traded price
switches to the new share basis while the latest eligible SEC-reported shares
may still correspond to the old basis.

Therefore, the final construction is conditional:

\[
MarketCap^{PIT}_{i,t} = RawClose_{i,t-1} \times Shares^{PIT}_{i,t},
\]

only when price and shares are on a compatible basis.

If a stock split has occurred after the reference date of the latest eligible
shares observation and on or before the relevant price date, the experiment
does not synthetically repair the shares series. Instead:

\[
MarketCap^{PIT}_{i,t} = \mathrm{NA},
\]

until a post-split reported-share observation becomes point-in-time eligible.
This deliberately sacrifices continuity rather than manufacture information
that was not observed under the frozen methodology.

### 4.3 Frozen Value representation

Once both components passed their respective point-in-time rules, Value could
be frozen as:

\[
\mathrm{Value}^{raw}_{i,t}
= \mathrm{EarningsYield}_{i,t}
= \frac{NI^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t}}
{RawClose_{i,t-1} \times Shares^{PIT}_{i,t}},
\]

subject to the price/share compatibility gate and the failure conditions of the
underlying TTM earnings reconstruction.

The final implementation explicitly rejects adjusted close for this Market Cap
construction and returns statuses such as `SPLIT_BASIS_MISMATCH` when the
denominator cannot be constructed coherently.

The Value investigation therefore produced a broader methodological result. A
familiar financial ratio is not automatically a valid historical
characteristic simply because all of its ingredients exist somewhere in the
dataset:

\[
\text{available fields}
\not\Rightarrow \text{compatible historical information}.
\]

Point-in-time validity requires both temporal availability and economic
compatibility between the quantities being combined.

## 5. Quality — When a Plausible Dimension Fails

The frozen raw representation is:

```text
Quality_raw = [Profitability, CashRealization]
```

Profitability and Cash Realization survived the PIT/accounting audit as
separate components. Balance-Sheet Resilience was rejected **for this
experiment**: no audited candidate attained sufficient PIT, accounting, and
cross-sector coherence. This does not say financial resilience is economically
irrelevant.

Quality began with a broader economic objective than a single accounting ratio.
The initial concept was to represent a firm's ability to generate economic
results efficiently, convert reported earnings into operating cash, and remain
financially resilient.

This motivated three candidate dimensions:

\[
\mathrm{Quality}^{candidate} =
[\mathrm{Profitability},
\mathrm{CashRealization},
\mathrm{BalanceSheetResilience}].
\]

Stage 1C did not assume that all three dimensions had to survive. Each was
audited separately under the same requirements of economic coherence,
point-in-time feasibility, reproducibility, and cross-firm interpretability.

The result was asymmetric: Profitability survived, Cash Realization survived
only after its initial representation was modified, and Balance-Sheet
Resilience was rejected for this experiment.

### 5.1 Profitability — efficiency relative to economic resources

The intended economic question was not simply whether a company earns a high
absolute profit. It was whether the company generates profit efficiently
relative to the economic resources supporting the business.

This made a return-on-assets-style construction more aligned with the
experiment than either an equity-only measure such as ROE or a sales-margin
measure.

The frozen representation became:

\[
\mathrm{Profitability}^{raw}_{i,t} =
\frac{NI^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t}}
{\left(Assets^{begin,PIT}_{i,t} + Assets^{end,PIT}_{i,t}\right) / 2}.
\]

The denominator is not simply the latest pair of available asset observations.
The two snapshots must correspond to the economic boundaries of the TTM
earnings interval and must themselves be point-in-time eligible.

Missing, stale, ambiguous, or incompatible boundary observations produce `NA`
rather than an imputed denominator.

Profitability was therefore frozen not because return on assets is universally
the correct definition of Quality, but because this construction survived the
experiment's economic and information-set requirements.

### 5.2 Cash Realization — when the raw ratio changes the question

The second dimension asks a different question:

> Given positive accounting earnings, to what extent are those earnings
> supported by operating cash generation?

The natural starting candidate was:

\[
\frac{OCF^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t}}{NI^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t}}.
\]

The operating-cash-flow audit showed that a PIT TTM series could be
reconstructed using the same broad quarterly logic applied to Net Income. The
problem was therefore not primarily data availability. It was the economic
behavior of the ratio itself.

Raw OCF/NI can become negative, unstable as earnings approach zero, and
arbitrarily large when operating cash flow exceeds reported earnings.

Most importantly, a value of 2 does not naturally mean that earnings are
“twice as realized” as a value of 1. Once operating cash generation fully
covers positive accounting earnings, the specific economic question posed by
this characteristic has already been satisfied.

The raw ratio was therefore not accepted unchanged. The frozen representation
became:

\[
\mathrm{CashRealization}_{i,t} =
\begin{cases}
\min\left(\dfrac{OCF^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t}}{NI^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t}}, 1\right), & NI^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t} > 0, \\
\mathrm{NA}, & NI^{TTM,PIT}_{i,t} \leq 0.
\end{cases}
\]

There is deliberately no lower cap at zero. If earnings are positive while
operating cash flow is negative, that economically adverse information remains
visible.

The upper cap serves a different purpose: values above one receive no
additional credit because full cash backing has already been achieved.

This was therefore not merely a numerical stabilization. The transformation
was dictated by the economic meaning assigned to the characteristic.

### 5.3 Balance-Sheet Resilience — economically relevant, empirically rejected

The third proposed Quality dimension was Balance-Sheet Resilience: the firm's
capacity to support its financial obligations.

The intended concept was explicitly not “less debt is always better.” A company
with more debt but substantially greater capacity to service that debt could be
more resilient than a company with less debt but weaker resources.

Several candidate representations were audited:

\[
\frac{OCF}{Debt}, \qquad
\frac{OCF}{NetDebt}, \qquad
\frac{OperatingIncome}{InterestExpense}.
\]

Here the audit exposed a more fundamental problem. The required accounting
objects were not economically homogeneous across the research universe.

Debt could appear as long-term debt, short-term borrowings, combined debt,
capital-lease obligations, deposits, or other funding structures. Cash
definitions were not universally available on the same narrow basis. Operating
Income and Interest Expense also lacked a sufficiently consistent construction
across the five audited issuers.

JPMorgan provided the decisive stress test. For a bank, deposits, borrowings,
interest flows, cash, and operating cash flow have a different economic role
from the corresponding concepts in an industrial company. Applying a universal
industrial-style leverage or debt-service ratio would therefore risk
interpreting business-model structure as financial fragility.

The conclusion was not that financial resilience is irrelevant to Quality. It
was narrower:

\[
\mathrm{BalanceSheetResilience}
\quad \text{— REJECTED FOR THIS EXPERIMENT}.
\]

No audited candidate provided a sufficiently coherent combination of
point-in-time availability, accounting construction, and cross-sector economic
interpretation.

### 5.4 Frozen Quality representation

The final raw Quality representation therefore contains only the two dimensions
that survived:

\[
\mathrm{Quality}^{raw}_{i,t} =
[\mathrm{Profitability}_{i,t},
\mathrm{CashRealization}_{i,t}].
\]

These two quantities are deliberately not yet combined into a scalar Quality
score. They have different economic meanings and different numerical scales.

Deciding how to transform and combine them belongs to the later construction of
\(X_{\mathrm{char}}\), not to Stage 1C.

Nor should this representation be interpreted as a claim that corporate
Quality consists only of profitability and cash realization. It means that
these are the Quality dimensions for which this experiment obtained a
sufficiently defensible raw representation under its frozen governance.

Quality therefore provides the clearest example so far of why the
characteristic layer was treated as research rather than feature collection:

\[
\text{initial economic concept}
\not\Rightarrow \text{all proposed dimensions must survive}.
\]

The research process was allowed to modify one candidate and reject another
before any downstream portfolio outcome was observed.

## 6. Growth — Separating Magnitude from Persistence

The frozen raw representation is:

```text
Growth_raw = [RevenueCAGR_3Y, PositiveAnnualGrowthFraction_3Y]
```

Magnitude and persistence carry distinct economic information: multi-year
expansion does not establish uninterrupted expansion. JPM's revenue-after-
interest-expense remains a banking activity measure with a cross-sector
interpretation limitation.

Growth represents sustained expansion in a company's economic activity. It is
not a claim that any single increase in revenue is inherently desirable, nor
that a high-growth company is necessarily a good investment. Instead, it asks
whether the business activity represented by the available reporting measure
has expanded over a medium-term horizon.

This requires separating two distinct properties of a revenue trajectory:

\[
\text{magnitude of growth}
\]

and

\[
\text{consistency of growth}.
\]

For example, the trajectories

\[
100 \rightarrow 110 \rightarrow 121 \rightarrow 133
\]

and

\[
100 \rightarrow 140 \rightarrow 105 \rightarrow 133
\]

can end at a similar level while describing materially different economic
paths. The first records sustained expansion; the second records expansion,
contraction, and subsequent recovery. A single endpoint-based measure does
not retain that distinction.

### 6.1 Revenue representation and point-in-time reconstruction

The Growth research used the issuer-specific revenue concepts that were found
to be economically interpretable and auditable in the Stage 1C audit:

| Issuer | Revenue concept |
| --- | --- |
| AAPL | `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax` |
| JPM | `RevenuesNetOfInterestExpense` |
| XOM | `Revenues` |
| UNH | `Revenues` |
| WMT | `Revenues` |

JPM's measure is retained with an explicit limitation.
`RevenuesNetOfInterestExpense` is a banking activity measure; it is not
accounting-identical to industrial sales or contract revenue. It is therefore
**conditionally coherent** for the Growth concept: it is an economically
interpretable issuer-level activity measure, but cross-sector comparisons must
retain this qualification.

Revenue TTM is reconstructed using the same compatible point-in-time quarterly
framework established for Net Income and Operating Cash Flow. Direct quarterly
facts are used where an appropriate standalone-quarter context exists;
otherwise compatible cumulative observations imply

\[
Q_2 = H1 - Q_1, \qquad
Q_3 = 9M - H1, \qquad
Q_4 = FY - 9M.
\]

Only facts with compatible issuer, accounting concept, unit, fiscal-period
structure, and filing/information vintage may be combined. Source accession,
form, acceptance timestamp, and eligibility date remain part of the
provenance. Amendments enter prospectively from their own point-in-time
eligibility date; later filings never rewrite an earlier information set.
Ambiguous, incompatible, or missing reconstruction inputs remain unavailable
rather than being silently repaired.

### 6.2 Growth magnitude

The frozen magnitude component uses a three-year trailing revenue horizon:

\[
\operatorname{RevenueCAGR\_3Y}_t
=
\left(
\frac{\operatorname{RevenueTTM}^{\mathrm{PIT}}_t}
     {\operatorname{RevenueTTM}^{\mathrm{PIT}}_{t-3y}}
\right)^{1/3}
-1.
\]

Three years is an ex ante, economically interpretable medium horizon for this
experiment. It was not selected using future returns, Sharpe ratios, backtest
performance, or downstream geometric results. A higher value denotes greater
annualized expansion of the PIT-available revenue measure over the period.

### 6.3 Growth persistence

Magnitude alone does not show whether expansion was sustained. Let four
compatible annual PIT revenue observations be

\[
R_{t-3},\ R_{t-2},\ R_{t-1},\ R_t,
\]

and define annual growth intervals

\[
g_1 = \frac{R_{t-2}}{R_{t-3}}-1, \qquad
g_2 = \frac{R_{t-1}}{R_{t-2}}-1, \qquad
g_3 = \frac{R_t}{R_{t-1}}-1.
\]

The persistence component is

\[
\operatorname{PositiveAnnualGrowthFraction\_3Y}_t
= \frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathbf{1}\{g_j>0\}.
\]

Its support is

\[
\left\{0,\ \frac{1}{3},\ \frac{2}{3},\ 1\right\}.
\]

A value of one means that each annual interval expanded; lower values record
one or more non-positive intervals. The component deliberately describes the
path of reported activity rather than the size of the final endpoint alone.

### 6.4 Missingness and continuity limitations

Both Growth components require a complete, compatible four-observation
three-year trajectory. If that trajectory is incomplete, ambiguous, or not
economically/accountingly comparable, both values are `NA`.

No manually adjusted "organic" revenue series is constructed. Acquisitions,
disposals, base effects, and changes in business perimeter are retained as
limitations of the reported measure. Likewise, a transition between reporting
tags is permitted only when there is sufficient economic and accounting
continuity to support it; otherwise the affected trajectory is unavailable.

### 6.5 Two raw Growth components, not one scalar Growth score

The frozen raw representation is

\[
\operatorname{Growth}_{\mathrm{raw},t}
=
\left[
\operatorname{RevenueCAGR\_3Y}_t,\
\operatorname{PositiveAnnualGrowthFraction\_3Y}_t
\right].
\]

The first component asks how much reported activity expanded; the second asks
how consistently it expanded. They are intentionally kept separate. No scalar
Growth score, normalization, ranking, or combination rule is defined here;
any later transformation or combination belongs to the construction of
\(X_{\mathrm{char}}\), not to this raw-component specification.

## 7. Momentum — The Same Data Field Can Be Right for One Object and Wrong for Another

The AAPL split sanity check distinguishes the objects. Adjusted close is
rejected for historical PIT Market Cap because of its mismatch with reported
share basis. It is accepted for return-based Momentum because it prevents a
mechanical split return, subject to an adjusted-price historical-vintage
limitation; Momentum does not claim SEC-like PIT provenance.

```text
Momentum_raw = [R_12M_AdjustedClose, R_1M_AdjustedClose]
```

`ProximityToHigh_12M` was investigated but not selected. Classical 12-1 is
not selected for this experiment's representation because it removes the recent
continuation/deterioration that the concept retains; this is not a claim that
12-1 is generally inferior.

Momentum differs from the preceding characteristics because its economic
information is contained directly in the evolution of market prices rather
than reconstructed from corporate filings.

The economic objective in Experiment #03 is to represent the strength of
price movement together with its recent persistence or deterioration.

This definition immediately creates two questions. First, which historical
price representation should be used when corporate actions mechanically alter
quoted prices? Second, should the most recent month be discarded, as in a
conventional 12-1 momentum construction, or retained because recent
continuation is explicitly part of the economic object being represented?

### 7.1 Corporate actions and the meaning of a return

The price field cannot be chosen independently of the object being measured.

Section 4 rejected historical adjusted close for the point-in-time Market Cap
denominator because retrospective split adjustments could make the price basis
incompatible with the latest eligible reported shares.

Momentum presents the opposite problem. Here the object of interest is the
economic price movement experienced by the security. A stock split changes the
quoted price mechanically without representing a corresponding loss of
shareholder value.

Using unadjusted prices across such an event would therefore manufacture a
return that did not economically occur.

The AAPL 4-for-1 split provides the sanity check. Across the split, raw close
implies an approximately

\[
-74.15\% 
\]

movement, while adjusted close implies approximately

\[
+3.39\%.
\]

The former is dominated by the mechanical change in share units rather than
the economic movement of the security. Adjusted close was therefore accepted
for Momentum.

This does not contradict its rejection for Market Cap. The same field is being
asked to represent two different economic objects:

\[
\mathrm{AdjustedClose} + \mathrm{Shares}^{\mathrm{PIT}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{basis incompatibility risk for Market Cap},
\]

whereas

\[
\mathrm{AdjustedClose\ through\ time}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{corporate-action-consistent price movement for Momentum}.
\]

Data validity is therefore conditional on the economic operation in which the
data participate.

### 7.2 Medium-term strength and recent continuation

A conventional momentum specification often removes the most recent month
from a longer lookback. That construction answers a legitimate empirical
question, but it is not identical to the one posed here.

Experiment #03 explicitly wants to distinguish medium-term price strength from
what has happened recently.

The frozen raw representation therefore retains both horizons:

\[
R^{12M,\mathrm{AdjClose}}_{i,t}
\]

and

\[
R^{1M,\mathrm{AdjClose}}_{i,t}.
\]

The twelve-month component represents broader price strength. The one-month
component provides information about recent continuation, deterioration, or
reversal relative to that broader movement.

Consequently,

\[
\mathrm{Momentum}^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,t}
=
\left[
R^{12M,\mathrm{AdjClose}}_{i,t},
R^{1M,\mathrm{AdjClose}}_{i,t}
\right].
\]

Classical 12-1 momentum was therefore not selected for this experiment's
representation because excluding the most recent month would deliberately
remove information that the frozen economic definition seeks to preserve.

This is not a claim that 12-1 momentum is generally inferior. It is a
statement about alignment between a measurement rule and the economic question
being asked.

### 7.3 Calendar horizons and the information cutoff

The decision clock remains the same as for the other characteristics. At the
XNYS open of \(t\), market information is available only through the previous
eligible session close.

The 12-month and 1-month reference dates are defined using calendar horizons
and mapped to the last eligible XNYS session, rather than approximated
mechanically using fixed numbers such as 252 and 21 observations.

If the required current or reference adjusted price is missing, non-positive,
or unavailable over the required horizon, Momentum returns `NA`. No shorter
substitute horizon is introduced.

### 7.4 A different level of point-in-time provenance

Momentum also exposes an important limitation that must remain explicit.

For SEC fundamentals, the experiment reconstructed historical information
using filing acceptance timestamps, prospective amendments, and accession-level
provenance.

The historical adjusted-close series used for Momentum does not provide
equivalent evidence that each adjusted value is exactly the version that would
have been available at every historical decision date.

Consequently, Momentum is accepted under a weaker historical-vintage assumption
than the fundamental characteristics:

\[
\text{current historical adjusted series}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{verified historical adjustment vintage}.
\]

The split sanity check supports the economic need for corporate-action
adjustment, but it does not eliminate this provenance limitation.

The experiment therefore does not claim SEC-like point-in-time provenance for
historical adjusted prices.

This distinction is intentional. Research governance does not require every
data source to possess identical provenance; it requires the strength of each
provenance claim to be stated accurately.

### 7.5 Candidate investigated but not selected

`ProximityToHigh_12M` was also investigated as a possible representation of
price strength. It was not selected for the frozen Momentum definition.

Its exclusion should not be interpreted as evidence that proximity to a high
contains no useful information.

The Stage 1C objective was not to exhaustively search for the strongest
predictive momentum feature, but to freeze a transparent representation
consistent with the economic definition before downstream portfolio outcomes
were observed.

### 7.6 Frozen Momentum representation

The final representation is

\[
\mathrm{Momentum}^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,t}
=
\left[
R^{12M,\mathrm{AdjClose}}_{i,t},
R^{1M,\mathrm{AdjClose}}_{i,t}
\right].
\]

The two components remain separate. A stock can display strong twelve-month
appreciation while its most recent month deteriorates, or weak broader
performance followed by a recent rebound.

Collapsing both observations into a scalar at this stage would discard
precisely the trajectory information the representation was designed to
preserve.

As with Quality and Growth, no normalization, weighting, ranking, or scalar
Momentum score is introduced in Stage 1C.

Momentum therefore reinforces a broader lesson from the characteristic audit:

\[
\text{a data field is not intrinsically ``correct'' or ``incorrect'';}\\
\text{its validity depends on the economic object being constructed.}
\]

## 8. What Did Not Survive the Research Process

The following are research results rather than mistakes:

- latest-restated historical fundamentals;
- adjusted close for PIT Market Cap;
- raw OCF/NI as a candidate that did not survive as the final representation and was modified into the frozen Cash Realization construction;
- a universal Balance-Sheet Resilience component;
- `ProximityToHigh_12M` as a selected Momentum component; and
- classical 12-1 as this experiment's final Momentum representation.

Stage 1C did not produce the frozen characteristic representation by simply selecting a set of plausible financial ratios and market features.

Several initially reasonable constructions failed, required modification, or were deliberately not selected once their economic meaning, temporal availability, accounting compatibility, or role in the experiment was examined.

These outcomes are part of the research result.

Importantly, however, not all exclusions mean the same thing. The audit produced at least three distinct outcomes:

\[
\text{candidate}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{MODIFIED + FROZEN}, \\
\text{REJECTED FOR THIS EXPERIMENT}, \\
\text{NOT SELECTED FOR THIS REPRESENTATION}.
\end{array}
\right.
\]

Treating these categories as equivalent would erase information about why a candidate did not enter the frozen raw representation.

### 8.1 Latest-restated fundamental history — rejected as an information model

A historical fundamental database can contain values that are currently known to be correct after subsequent amendments or restatements.

That does not mean those values belonged to the information set available at the historical portfolio decision.

The experiment therefore rejected a latest-restated view of history as the basis for the fundamental characteristics. Amendments enter the research information set prospectively rather than rewriting earlier decisions.

This exclusion is more fundamental than rejecting an individual factor formula. It defines what counts as historical information in the experiment:

\[
\text{historical research state}
\ne
\text{latest known version of the past}.
\]

The resulting PIT framework preserves the distinction between accounting observation period, filing acceptance, and portfolio eligibility.

### 8.2 Adjusted close for Market Cap — rejected for one operation, retained for another

Adjusted close did not survive the construction of point-in-time Market Cap.

Retrospective corporate-action adjustments can place historical price on a different share basis from the latest PIT-eligible SEC shares, making their product economically inconsistent.

It was therefore replaced by raw close together with an explicit split-basis compatibility gate.

But adjusted close was not rejected as a data field in general. It was later retained for Momentum because return measurement across stock splits requires corporate-action consistency.

This distinction matters:

\[
\text{invalid for one construction}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{invalid data universally}.
\]

The research object determines whether the representation is coherent.

### 8.3 Raw OCF / Net Income — modified rather than discarded

The original Cash Realization candidate was

\[
\frac{OCF^{TTM,PIT}}{NI^{TTM,PIT}}.
\]

It survived neither economically nor numerically in its unrestricted form. It could become negative, unstable near zero positive earnings, and arbitrarily large above one.

More importantly, values above one did not have a proportional interpretation under the specific question being asked.

The underlying economic idea was not rejected. Its representation was modified:

\[
\mathrm{CashRealization}
=
\min\left(
\frac{OCF^{TTM,PIT}}{NI^{TTM,PIT}},
1
\right),
\qquad NI^{TTM,PIT}>0,
\]

and

\[
\mathrm{CashRealization}=\mathrm{NA},
\qquad NI^{TTM,PIT}\leq0.
\]

There is no lower cap.

This is therefore a

\[
\text{MODIFIED + FROZEN}
\]

result, not a rejected characteristic.

### 8.4 Balance-Sheet Resilience — rejected for this experiment

Balance-Sheet Resilience produced a different outcome.

The economic idea remained meaningful, but the investigated representations

\[
\frac{OCF}{Debt}, \qquad
\frac{OCF}{NetDebt}, \qquad
\frac{OperatingIncome}{InterestExpense}
\]

did not provide a sufficiently coherent universal construction across the audited universe.

Differences in debt definitions, funding structures, cash concepts, and the economics of financial versus non-financial firms prevented the experiment from freezing a representation without introducing additional modeling assumptions.

JPMorgan provided the clearest stress case: applying an industrial-style debt-service representation to a bank risks treating the structure of the banking business itself as financial weakness.

The result therefore remained

\[
\mathrm{BalanceSheetResilience}
\quad\text{— REJECTED FOR THIS EXPERIMENT}.
\]

This does not imply that financial resilience is irrelevant or impossible to model. It means that this experiment did not obtain a defensible common representation under its frozen requirements.

### 8.5 12-1 Momentum and Proximity-to-High — not selected

The Momentum investigation generated a third type of exclusion.

Conventional 12-1 Momentum was not selected because this experiment explicitly wanted recent price behavior to remain visible. Removing the latest month would answer a different momentum question from the one frozen here.

`ProximityToHigh_12M` was also investigated but not selected for the final raw representation.

Neither decision constitutes evidence that these features are economically invalid or predictively inferior. They are therefore best classified as

\[
\text{NOT SELECTED FOR THIS REPRESENTATION}
\]

rather than rejected.

### 8.6 Rejection is itself a research result

The purpose of Stage 1C was not to maximize the number of characteristics that survived. Nor was it to search across alternative definitions until every economic idea could be represented.

Its purpose was to determine which representations could be defended before observing downstream portfolio or return outcomes.

This creates an important asymmetry:

- a candidate could survive unchanged;
- an economic idea could survive while its initial representation was modified;
- a plausible dimension could fail the experiment's requirements; or
- an alternative could remain valid but simply answer a different question.

None of these outcomes was determined by Sharpe ratio, alpha, portfolio performance, or the later geometric decomposition.

That distinction is central to the governance of Experiment #03:

\[
\text{failure to enter }X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{evidence of predictive failure}.
\]

At this stage, the experiment has evaluated representation, not alpha.

## 9. Frozen Raw Representation

After completing the individual characteristic audits, Stage 1C leaves a
frozen set of seven raw components organized under four economic concepts:

\[
X_{\mathrm{char},t}^{\mathrm{raw}}
=
\left[
EY_t,
\mathrm{Profitability}_t,
\mathrm{CashRealization}_t,
\mathrm{RevenueCAGR}_{3Y,t},
\mathrm{PositiveAnnualGrowthFraction}_{3Y,t},
R^{12M,\mathrm{AdjClose}}_t,
R^{1M,\mathrm{AdjClose}}_t
\right].
\]

For an \(N\)-asset universe, this is a valid raw characteristic
representation:

\[
X_{\mathrm{char},t}^{\mathrm{raw}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times 7}.
\]

The seven columns are economically grouped as

\[
EY \longrightarrow \mathrm{Value},
\]

\[
[\mathrm{Profitability},\ \mathrm{CashRealization}]
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Quality},
\]

\[
[\mathrm{RevenueCAGR}_{3Y},\ \mathrm{PositiveAnnualGrowthFraction}_{3Y}]
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Growth},
\]

and

\[
[R_{12M},\ R_{1M}] \longrightarrow \mathrm{Momentum}.
\]

This inventory is the output of Stage 1C.

### 9.1 Seven measurements are not yet four portfolio characteristics

The notation Value, Quality, Growth, and Momentum can make the representation
appear more complete than it actually is.

Value currently has one raw component, while Quality, Growth, and Momentum
each have two:

\[
V^{\mathrm{raw}}=[EY],
\]

\[
Q^{\mathrm{raw}}=[\mathrm{Profitability},\ \mathrm{CashRealization}],
\]

\[
G^{\mathrm{raw}}=[\mathrm{RevenueCAGR}_{3Y},\ \mathrm{PositiveAnnualGrowthFraction}_{3Y}],
\]

and

\[
M^{\mathrm{raw}}=[R_{12M},\ R_{1M}].
\]

Stage 1C has therefore not yet produced four directly comparable Value,
Quality, Growth, and Momentum columns. Nor has it determined how multiple
components within Quality, Growth, or Momentum should eventually be combined.

### 9.2 The raw columns are intentionally heterogeneous

The seven measurements do not naturally live on a common numerical scale.

Earnings Yield and Profitability are ratios. Cash Realization has an
economically motivated upper cap and can remain negative. Revenue CAGR is a
continuous growth rate. Growth Persistence belongs to the discrete set

\[
\left\{0,\ \frac{1}{3},\ \frac{2}{3},\ 1\right\}.
\]

The two Momentum components are historical returns.

Their missingness mechanisms also differ. A fundamental observation can be
unavailable because of filing chronology, accounting ambiguity, insufficient
quarterly history, or incompatible economic boundaries. Market Cap can fail a
split-basis compatibility gate. Momentum can be unavailable because the
required price history is missing.

Consequently, placing the seven columns next to each other does not make them
directly comparable.

### 9.3 Raw representation is not the final characteristic matrix

This distinction is sufficiently important to state explicitly:

\[
X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}} \ne X_{\mathrm{char}}.
\]

Stage 1C determined what economic quantities survived the representation audit.
It did not determine:

- cross-sectional transformations;
- normalization or scaling;
- treatment of outliers;
- the downstream handling of missing observations;
- how the two Quality components should be combined;
- how the two Growth components should be combined;
- how the two Momentum components should be combined; or
- the relative importance of Value, Quality, Growth, and Momentum.

None of those choices should be silently inferred from the existence of
\(X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}}\).

### 9.4 What has and has not been frozen

The research has therefore crossed a specific boundary.

It has frozen

\[
\text{economic meaning}
+
\text{raw measurement rule}
+
\text{information-set discipline}
+
\text{known failure conditions}.
\]

It has not frozen

\[
\text{transformation}
+
\text{normalization}
+
\text{aggregation}
+
\text{portfolio mapping}.
\]

In particular, no ranking, z-score, winsorization, clipping beyond already
frozen characteristic-specific rules, composite score, or portfolio weight
should be read into the raw representation.

### 9.5 The endpoint of Stage 1C

This distinction prevents a subtle methodological shortcut.

It would be easy to take the seven columns, standardize them, average them into
four scores, combine those scores, and call the result a quantamental
portfolio. But every operation in that sequence changes the representation.

Stage 1C therefore stops one step earlier. Its output is not yet a portfolio
and not yet the final matrix that will enter the portfolio mapping \(g(\cdot)\).

Its output is a set of economically defined and auditable raw measurements
from which that later representation can be constructed.

In compact form:

\[
\text{Stage 1C: Economic Ideas}
\;\longrightarrow\;
X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}}.
\]

The transformations required to obtain \(X_{\mathrm{char}}\) remain a
subsequent research problem.

## 10. From Representation Back to Geometry

The future research chain is:

```text
X_char_raw
  -> X_char
  -> g(X_char)
  -> w_raw
  -> F_risk
  -> (w_parallel, w_perp)
```

This note does not solve any step in that chain. Stage 2 — Freeze universe is
the next stage, but it is not authorized or started by this note.

Experiment #03 began with a geometric question:

> How much of a quantamental portfolio's structure is genuinely independent of the systematic risk exposures already known to us?

But the geometric decomposition developed in #01 and #02 assumes that the object being decomposed already exists.

Before asking what component of \(w_{\mathrm{raw}}\) lies inside or outside a risk subspace, the experiment first had to establish what economic information would generate that portfolio.

Stage 1C therefore moved temporarily away from projection geometry and toward representation.

The result is the frozen raw object:

\[
X_{\mathrm{char},t}^{\mathrm{raw}}
=
\left[
EY,
\mathrm{Profitability},
\mathrm{CashRealization},
\mathrm{RevenueCAGR}_{3Y},
\mathrm{PositiveAnnualGrowthFraction}_{3Y},
R_{12M},
R_{1M}
\right]_t.
\]

This is not a detour from the geometric experiment. It is a prerequisite for making that experiment interpretable.

### 10.1 The remaining bridge

The research sequence can now be written as

\[
\text{Economic Ideas}
\;\longrightarrow\;
X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\left[\text{Representation Decisions — not yet resolved}\right]
\;\longrightarrow\;
X_{\mathrm{char}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
g(X_{\mathrm{char}})
\;\longrightarrow\;
w_{\mathrm{raw}}.
\]

Stage 1C has reached only the second object in this sequence.

The transformations needed to move from heterogeneous raw measurements to \(X_{\mathrm{char}}\), and the mapping

\[
w_{\mathrm{raw}}=g(X_{\mathrm{char}}),
\]

remain deliberately unresolved.

Consequently, #03A does not yet contain a quantamental portfolio.

### 10.2 The risk representation is a separate object

Once \(w_{\mathrm{raw}}\) eventually exists, the experiment will require another representation:

\[
F_{\mathrm{risk},t}
=
[f_1,\ldots,f_M]
\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times M}.
\]

This matrix is conceptually different from \(X_{\mathrm{char}}\).

\(X_{\mathrm{char}}\) describes the economic characteristics used to construct the portfolio. \(F_{\mathrm{risk}}\) will describe selected systematic exposure directions against which that portfolio is analyzed.

The methodology therefore keeps the two objects separate rather than assuming that a characteristic used to construct a portfolio is automatically the same thing as a risk direction.

The exact contents of \(F_{\mathrm{risk}}\) are not selected in #03A.

### 10.3 Returning to the geometry

Only after both \(w_{\mathrm{raw}}\) and \(F_{\mathrm{risk}}\) have been defined does the original geometric experiment become operational. Let

\[
S_{\mathrm{risk}}
=
\operatorname{Col}(F_{\mathrm{risk}})
\subseteq \mathbb{R}^{N}.
\]

Then the portfolio can be decomposed as

\[
w_{\mathrm{raw}}
=
P_{S_{\mathrm{risk}}}w_{\mathrm{raw}}
+
(I-P_{S_{\mathrm{risk}}})w_{\mathrm{raw}}
=
w_{\parallel}+w_{\perp}.
\]

The first component,

\[
w_{\parallel}=P_{S_{\mathrm{risk}}}w_{\mathrm{raw}},
\]

represents the part of the portfolio lying in the span of the selected known risk directions. The second,

\[
w_{\perp}=(I-P_{S_{\mathrm{risk}}})w_{\mathrm{raw}},
\]

represents the component orthogonal to those directions under the chosen inner product.

At that point the original research question can finally be investigated empirically:

> How much of the quantamental portfolio survives outside \(S_{\mathrm{risk}}\)?

### 10.4 What "survives" will and will not mean

Even then, \(w_{\perp}\) should not automatically be interpreted as alpha.

Orthogonality to the selected columns of \(F_{\mathrm{risk}}\) establishes a geometric property relative to the specified risk subspace. It does not establish predictive power, positive expected return, economic profitability, or robustness out of sample.

Likewise, a large \(w_{\parallel}\) would not prove that the original quantamental construction was useless. It would show that a meaningful portion of its structure can be represented by the selected known risk directions.

The eventual geometric experiment is therefore diagnostic before it is predictive.

### 10.5 What #03A has established

The contribution of #03A is consequently narrower, but foundational.

It established that before decomposing a portfolio into known and orthogonal directions, the portfolio's economic inputs themselves must be treated as research objects.

The path was

\[
\text{Economic Hypothesis}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{Candidate Representation}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{PIT Audit}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{Falsification / Limitation}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{Frozen Raw Representation}.
\]

That process produced seven frozen raw measurements, while also documenting representations that were modified, rejected, or deliberately not selected.

It did not produce \(X_{\mathrm{char}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{raw}}\), \(F_{\mathrm{risk}}\), a neutralized portfolio, or evidence of alpha.

This is precisely the boundary of the note.

#03A ends with the economic representation sufficiently defined to continue the experiment, but before the choices required to transform that representation into a portfolio are made.

The research can now return to geometry — but only after those intermediate decisions are investigated and frozen with the same discipline used to construct \(X_{\mathrm{char}}^{\mathrm{raw}}\).
